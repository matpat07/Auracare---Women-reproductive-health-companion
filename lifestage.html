🌸 Teen & First Period — Your Body, Your Power

Everything here is private to your device. No names, no judgment, just facts and support.

🩸 What is a Period?

A period (menstruation) is when the uterus sheds its lining. It usually lasts 3–7 days and comes once a month. It's completely normal — every woman has one.

📆 Will it be regular?

In the first 1–2 years, periods are often irregular — that's normal! Stress, weight changes, and illness can also shift dates. Use the Cycle Tracker to log yours.

🛡️ Privacy First

All your data stays on this device only. No cloud, no sharing. You control everything.

😟 Is it supposed to hurt?

Mild cramps are normal. Severe pain that stops you from daily activities is not — talk to a trusted adult or doctor. You deserve to be pain-free.

🧴 Products 101

Pads, tampons, menstrual cups, and period underwear all work. There's no "right" choice — use whatever feels comfortable for you.

💬 Need to talk?

Aura AI is available 24/7 and completely anonymous. Start a conversation →

🌟 Remember

Your period does not define you. It's one small part of who you are. You are powerful, worthy, and capable — at every phase of your cycle.

🌙 Menopause Mode

Supporting you through perimenopause and menopause — hot flashes, sleep changes, mood, and beyond.

🌡️ Hot Flashes

Sudden warmth spreading from chest to face is a vasomotor symptom. What helps: cool water, layered clothing, paced breathing (slow exhale). Tracking triggers (spicy food, caffeine, stress) helps predict them.

😴 Sleep Changes

Night sweats and insomnia are common. Keep your room cool (18–20°C), avoid screens 1 hour before bed, and try magnesium-rich foods. Log sleep quality to share with your doctor.

😔 Mood & Brain Fog

Estrogen withdrawal affects serotonin. Mood swings, irritability, and forgetfulness are real and valid. Talk to Aura AI → or use the breathing exercises in Resources.

🩸 Cycle Changes

Perimenopause can cause very irregular periods — more frequent, less frequent, or heavier. Keep logging your cycle. Any bleeding 12+ months after your last period should be discussed with your doctor.
